RBF kernel ridge:

K(x,x′)=exp[−γ‖z(x)−z(x′)‖²]
ŷ=clip(β₀+Σⱼβⱼφⱼ(z(x)),0,1)

The scalable kernel uses median imputation, standardized features, 384 seeded random Fourier features, and training-CV-selected γ and ridge α. Histogram gradient boosting is an additive tree ensemble and therefore has no single linear coefficient equation.

Detailed methodology

Inputs and coordinate matching

  1. Five cell lines: A549, HCT116, HepG2, K562, and MCF7.
  2. Technical sequencing runs belonging to the same biological replicate are summed at raw-count level.
  3. All junctions use GRCh38 0-based half-open intron coordinates plus strand. LeafCutter’s end coordinate is converted before matching.
  4. Exact junction support requires a positive long-read count in at least one biological replicate. Zero support is retained as non-observation rather than called biological absence.

Normalization

  • MAJIQ: short-read PSI is compared with long-read junction count divided by total long-read counts in the identical MAJIQ choice set/source-target context.
  • LeafCutter: junction count is divided by total junction count in the identical LeafCutter cluster, independently by biological replicate.
  • Combined: ML concatenates the allowed MAJIQ and LeafCutter predictors. No long-read count, denominator, range, error, event class, or response-derived label is a predictor.
  • CPM clarification: CPM divides by total usable library reads; it is not gene-wise normalization. Gene-junction fraction is gene-relative but only available for uniquely assigned annotated genes.
  • Biological-replicate normalized values are summarized by mean, SD, minimum, maximum, and usable-replicate count. Replicates receive equal weight; zero denominators remain NA without pseudocounts.

Training and validation

  1. A shared 55,666-row universe ensures MAJIQ-only, LeafCutter-only, and combined models are evaluated on identical junction/context rows.
  2. 45,609 rows are training and 10,057 are validation. Connected components link genes, choice sets, contexts, junctions, and LeafCutter clusters so related events do not cross the primary split.
  3. Four-fold GroupKFold cross-validation on training components selects algorithms and hyperparameters by MAE.
  4. The held-out validation split is used once for final MAE, RMSE, R², Pearson, Spearman, and error-threshold reporting.
  5. Uncertainty uses the 90th percentile of absolute out-of-fold training residuals. Ridge-versus-primary-model disagreement contributes to manual-review priority.
  6. The strict sensitivity analysis retains only events where MAJIQ choice-set and LeafCutter cluster junction membership are exactly equal.

How MAJIQ and LeafCutter are combined

In the no-ML section, combination means a simple 50:50 arithmetic consensus on common rows. In ML, combination means concatenating all ten MAJIQ summaries and ten LeafCutter summaries; the algorithm learns any weighting or nonlinear interaction exclusively from training data.

Interpretation and limitations

  • No fully independent laboratory, tissue, disease cohort, alignment protocol, or sequencing protocol has been tested.
  • Leave-one-cell-line-out testing holds out the cell line but may still expose the model to the same genes or junction identities in other cell lines.
  • The response is long-read local junction usage in a MAJIQ context, not proof of complete transcript isoform correctness.
  • LeafCutter and MAJIQ can define different denominators; use strict-sensitivity results when denominator identity is essential.
  • Bambu provenance and transcript-level Bambu validation are absent from this dataset.
  • Genome-build errors, mapping artifacts, paralogs, sample swaps, very low coverage, and novel biology may still require IGV/UCSC inspection.
